# Video Edit Styles — Master Prompt for Claude

Two styles. Tell Claude which one when you post raw footage — or let it pick:
explainer / documentary topic = Style 1 (Sumit), numbered tips / listicle = Style 2 (Kelsie).

## STYLE 1 — Sumit (@sumit.visions)

Style reference: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfRAkn4xIZy/ ("Claude vs Human Creativity").
The HUMAN edit in that reel is the target. The Claude edit in the same reel is what NOT to copy.

### Style bible

1. Talking head stays on screen. Graphics support the speaker, they never replace them.
2. One color theme per video (his reel used pink). Backgrounds, text, icons and
   illustrations all live in that one palette.
3. One visual per spoken beat. Every idea gets a kinetic-text keyword plus a simple
   icon or illustration of the metaphor in the words (brain = overthinking, shield =
   protection, compass/target = goals). No visual without a spoken reason behind it.
4. Fast, clean pacing. A new visual on nearly every beat. No dead air, no empty
   talking-head stretches.
5. Real photos only when they prove something (a diploma, a book cover, a result).
6. Keywords big, bold, animated in exactly on the beat of the word being spoken.
7. Documentary tone: clear, confident, educational. Never cheesy, never cluttered.

### Detailed build process — 6 steps, 7 days (from the Overstimulated Mind edit process)

1. **Hook & setup (Day 0–1).** Analyze the raw talking head, craft the opening hook
   ("There is a version OF THIS VIDEO"), sync the speaking audio, pick the emphasis
   words ("Lazy", "undisciplined", "PHONE"). Set the theme: one glow color
   (orange/brown here, pink in the Olga reel), scattered typography, stark
   grayscale background shifts. Tools: Premiere Pro, Notion, Google Docs.
2. **Masking & roto (Day 1–2).** Roto Brush the speaker out of the background. Animated
   neon outline in the theme color around their exact silhouette. Keyframe the
   background from the natural room to stark white/grayscale for contrast. Clean mask
   edges around hair and hands. Tools: After Effects, Photoshop.
3. **B-roll & PiP collages (Day 2–3).** Secondary footage for multi-cam sequences
   (driving, lounging, walking outdoors). Picture-in-picture collages with drop
   shadows, several clips on screen at once. Prep 3D assets for metaphor cues (e.g.
   dartboard icon for "discipline"). Tools: Premiere Pro, After Effects.
4. **Kinetic typography (Day 3–5).** Bold text popping word-by-word, synced to the
   speaker's pacing. Scattered-text effect for key phrases ("and push through things"),
   words placed randomly across the screen. Mixed fonts: serif for accents ("Lazy"),
   clean sans-serif for subtitles. Alternate theme color and white. Scale bounces and
   rotational keyframes for energy. Tools: After Effects.
5. **SFX & color polish (Day 5–6).** Whooshes for collage slide-ins, a pop/click on
   every kinetic word reveal. Balance voiceover EQ so SFX never overpower speech.
   Warm color grade in the theme color, natural skin tones kept. Tools: Audition,
   After Effects, Premiere Pro.
6. **Review & export (Day 6–7).** Final playback check that text and collages are
   perfectly timed. Apply revisions. Export H.264 MP4 optimized for social. Back up
   project files to cloud. Tools: Media Encoder, Frame.io, Google Drive.

## STYLE 2 — Kelsie (@_k.elsie, Kelsie Blevins, REALTOR)

Style reference: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DewTzCzxR9q/
"Thinking about selling? Here's everything you need to know in under 30 seconds."

### Style bible

1. The hook is a promise plus a visible clock. Open with "everything you need to know
   in under 30 seconds" and keep a countdown timer on screen the whole video — the
   ticking clock is the pressure that keeps people watching.
2. Numbered rapid-fire list. 12 tips in ~35 seconds, about 3 seconds per tip. One
   numbered badge per tip, counting up on screen as the list runs.
3. Animated keyword text overlays on every tip. Short phrases only ("Don't overprice",
   "Marketing Plan", "Strategy") — big, bold, popping in on the beat. Never full
   sentences on screen.
4. Seated talking head, direct to camera, bright clean interior. The speaker is the
   authority — confident, instructional, promotional.
5. One idea per tip, no rambling. Cut ruthlessly: if a tip takes more than one
   sentence to say, it is two tips or it gets cut.
6. End card with two calls to action: "Save this for later" and the contact CTA
   (DM / call). The save prompt is the point — it is what makes the reel spread.
7. Caption carries the full numbered list so the video works even on mute and people
   save it as a checklist.

### Listing tour (Kelsie) — the Wilmore format

Style reference: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfzaFE1xv02/
2232 Wilmore Dr, Charlotte, $649,950 — 53-second listing tour (Nov 16, 2026).

1. Hook on lifestyle first, not the house. Open walking through the neighborhood
   (market, coffee in hand) with "What if this? and this? minutes from your door"
   — sell where you live before what you buy.
2. Fly-through push-in zoom. The camera zooms from the exterior straight into the
   interior in one continuous move — street to front door to rooms, no cut. That
   single unbroken push is what makes the house feel big and the tour feel premium.
   Recreate with a drone/gimbal push-in, or a zoom transition in the edit.
3. Aerial skyline shot to sell the neighborhood. One wide drone shot over the area
   (skyline, trees, streets) right after the hook, before the house itself.
4. Room-by-room tour, best feature per room, no lingering. Porch piece-to-camera
   ("this is where you want to be"), kitchen, open living/dining, bedrooms, then
   backyard + deck + fire pit. One selling point per room, then move on.
5. AI home transformation clip. An AI-generated shot where the house turns/rotates
   while its look changes — a restyle/renovation morph. Generate with an AI video
   tool (Runway, Kling, Higgsfield, Luma): image-to-video turntable of the house,
   then morph its style/era. It is the "wow" beat of the tour.
6. Close seated inside the home with the investment line ("If location is
   everything in real estate, this one is a smart investment") and a comment-word
   CTA ("Comment WILMORE for more info").

### Vision pitch (Melissa Orta) — the redevelopment format

Style reference: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfkBM9XuE_L/
Melissa Orta (@melissaortarealtor, Marzucco Real Estate), Lehigh Acres FL — 27-second
"If this were my property" pitch (Nov 10, 2026).

1. Hook on location, direct to camera. Stand on the site itself and open with the
   "if this were mine" line ("If this were my property, I'd knock it down and start
   over") — the speaker is the vision, not a narrator.
2. Drone aerial of the current site — the "before." Wide shot of the empty/vacant
   property (industrial buildings, cracked lots) so the viewer feels the wasted
   potential.
3. AI-render overlay on the aerial — the "after." AI-generated visuals laid over the
   drone shot showing the transformed complex: a coffee shop with big windows and
   outdoor seating, a brunch spot next door, an Italian restaurant for date night.
   The render is the wow beat — same trick as the Kelsie AI home transformation,
   applied to a whole development instead of one house.
4. Name each concept with on-screen text as you narrate it. One short label per
   idea, appearing on the beat you say it — never a paragraph on screen.
5. Return to the ground, gesture at the open space, and ask the viewers what
   they would want to see there. The question is the engagement engine — it turns
   a pitch into a conversation in the comments.
6. Close with the branding logo. No hard sell, no phone number on screen — the
   authority and the question do the work.

   countdown timer and the numbered badges.

   throughout? One theme only (Sumit) / timer and numbers on every tip (Kelsie)?
   Keywords spelled right? Pacing tight with no dead air? End card with the save +
   contact CTA (Kelsie)?

## Rules (both styles)

- Only visualize what was actually said. Never invent quotes, facts or claims.
- Never leave a plain talking-head stretch longer than a few seconds without a visual.
- Keep the speaker's framing clean — graphics sit around them, never covering their face.
- Ask me a question only if the footage has no usable audio or the topic is unclear.
  Otherwise proceed and deliver.
- Tell me which tool you used for the build (After Effects MCP, MotionSmith, or other).

## What I will give you each time

Raw footage file, topic, style (Sumit or Kelsie), target duration, platform
(Reels / TikTok / Shorts).

## What you give back

The edited video file, plus a one-line summary: the style, the theme color or the tip
count, and the beat list. For Kelsie style, also the numbered-list caption.
