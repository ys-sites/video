Documentary explainer

Style 1 — The Sumit Style

Sumit · Video editor

What it looks like: The speaker stays on camera the whole time. Every idea they speak gets a visual: a keyword in big animated text plus a simple illustration of the metaphor. One colour runs through the whole video — pink in the reference reel. Fast and clean, never boring.

Best for: Explainers, lessons, talking-head teaching videos — anything that teaches one idea well.

  • Speaker stays on screen. Graphics support them, they never replace them.
  • One colour theme per video. Backgrounds, text, icons and illustrations all live in that one palette.
  • One visual per spoken beat. Every idea gets a kinetic-text keyword plus a simple icon of the metaphor (brain = overthinking, shield = protection, compass or target = goals). No visual without a spoken reason behind it.
  • Fast, clean pacing. A new visual on nearly every beat. No dead air, no empty talking-head stretches.
  • Real photos only when they prove something — a diploma, a book cover, a result.
  • Keywords big, bold, animated in exactly on the beat of the word being spoken.
  • Documentary tone: clear, confident, educational. Never cheesy, never cluttered.
1
Day 0–1

Hook & setup

Study the raw talking head, write the opening hook, sync the speaking audio, pick the emphasis words, and set the theme: one glow colour, scattered typography, stark grayscale background shifts.

Tools: DaVinci Resolve, Notion, Google Docs

2
Day 1–2

Masking & roto

Cut the speaker out of the background with Roto Brush. Add an animated neon outline in the theme colour around their exact silhouette. Keyframe the background from the natural room to stark white or grayscale. Clean mask edges around hair and hands.

Tools: Remotion, GIMP

3
Day 2–3

B-roll & picture-in-picture

Secondary footage for multi-cam sequences (driving, lounging, walking outdoors). Picture-in-picture collages with drop shadows, several clips on screen at once. Prep 3D assets for metaphor cues (e.g. a dartboard icon for discipline).

Tools: DaVinci Resolve, Remotion

4
Day 3–5

Kinetic typography

Bold text popping word by word, synced to the speaker's pacing. Scattered-text effect for key phrases, words placed randomly across the screen. Mixed fonts: serif for accents, clean sans-serif for subtitles. Alternate theme colour and white. Scale bounces and rotational keyframes for energy.

Tools: Remotion

5
Day 5–6

Sound & colour polish

Whooshes for collage slide-ins, a pop or click on every kinetic word reveal. Balance voiceover EQ so sound effects never overpower speech. Warm colour grade in the theme colour, natural skin tones kept.

Tools: Audacity, Remotion, DaVinci Resolve

6
Day 6–7

Review & export

Final playback check that text and collages are perfectly timed. Apply revisions. Export H.264 MP4 optimised for social. Back up project files to the cloud.

Tools: ffmpeg, Frame.io, Google Drive

"Claude vs Human Creativity" — the same talking-head clip edited three ways. The human edit is the target; the Claude edit in the same reel is what NOT to copy.

30-second listicle

Style 2 — The Kelsie Listicle

Kelsie Blevins · Top 1% realtor, North Carolina

What it looks like: A numbered list, fired fast. A promise plus a visible clock: "everything you need to know in under 30 seconds" with a ticking countdown timer on screen the whole time. Twelve tips in about 35 seconds, one big keyword flashing per tip.

Best for: Tips lists, checklists, real estate, any "X things to know" video.

  • The hook is a promise plus a visible clock. Open with "everything you need to know in under 30 seconds" and keep a countdown timer on screen the whole video — the ticking clock is the pressure that keeps people watching.
  • Numbered rapid-fire list. 12 tips in about 35 seconds, roughly 3 seconds per tip. One numbered badge per tip, counting up on screen as the list runs.
  • Animated keyword text overlays on every tip. Short phrases only ("Don't overprice", "Marketing Plan", "Strategy") — big, bold, popping in on the beat. Never full sentences on screen.
  • Seated talking head, direct to camera, bright clean interior. The speaker is the authority — confident, instructional, promotional.
  • One idea per tip, no rambling. Cut ruthlessly: if a tip takes more than one sentence to say, it is two tips or it gets cut.
  • End card with two calls to action: "Save this for later" and the contact CTA (DM / call). The save prompt is the point — it is what makes the reel spread.
  • Caption carries the full numbered list so the video works even on mute and people save it as a checklist.
1

Write the list

Pick 12 tips. One sentence each, one idea each. If a tip needs two sentences, split it or cut it.

2

Set the clock

Put a visible 30-second countdown timer on screen for the whole video. It is the hook.

3

Film seated, direct to camera

Bright, clean interior. Confident delivery, about 3 seconds per tip.

4

Add numbered badges and keywords

A counting badge per tip, plus one big animated keyword phrase per tip — short phrases only.

5

End card and caption

End card: "Save this for later" plus the contact CTA. Write the full numbered list into the caption as a checklist.

Home tour

Style 3 — Kelsie Listing Tour — The Wilmore Format

Kelsie Blevins · Top 1% realtor, North Carolina

What it looks like: A lifestyle-first home tour: sell where you live before what you buy. One unbroken camera flight from the street into the rooms, an aerial of the neighbourhood, then room by room — one selling point each, no lingering — and an AI clip where the house turns and changes its look.

Best for: Selling a home, listing tours, property walkthroughs.

1

Hook on lifestyle first, not the house

Open walking through the neighbourhood (market, coffee in hand): "What if this? and this? minutes from your door." Sell where you live before what you buy.

2

Fly-through push-in zoom

The camera zooms from the exterior straight into the interior in one continuous move — street to front door to rooms, no cut. That single unbroken push is what makes the house feel big and the tour feel premium. Recreate with a drone or gimbal push-in, or a zoom transition in the edit.

Tools: Drone / gimbal, Zoom transition

3

Aerial skyline shot

One wide drone shot over the area (skyline, trees, streets) right after the hook, before the house itself.

Tools: Drone

4

Room-by-room tour

Best feature per room, no lingering. Porch piece-to-camera ("this is where you want to be"), kitchen, open living and dining, bedrooms, then backyard + deck + fire pit. One selling point per room, then move on.

5

AI home transformation clip

An AI-generated shot where the house turns and rotates while its look changes — a restyle or renovation morph. Generate with an AI video tool (ComfyUI, ComfyUI, ComfyUI, ComfyUI): image-to-video turntable of the house, then morph its style or era. It is the "wow" beat of the tour.

Tools: ComfyUI, ComfyUI, ComfyUI, ComfyUI

6

Close with the investment line

Close seated inside the home: "If location is everything in real estate, this one is a smart investment" — plus a comment-word CTA ("Comment WILMORE for more info").

Redevelopment pitch

Style 5 — The Melissa Vision Pitch

Melissa Orta · Broker Associate, Marzucco Real Estate — Lehigh Acres, FL

What it looks like: The agent stands on the site itself and pitches what it could become. Drone footage shows the empty lot as it is today; AI renders laid over the aerial show the future complex. The video ends by asking viewers what they would want to see there.

Best for: Pitching a vision for a property or place; redevelopment ideas; getting a community talking about what a site could become.

1

Hook on location, direct to camera

Stand on the site itself and open with the "if this were mine" line ("If this were my property, I'd knock it down and start over"). The speaker is the vision, not a narrator.

2

Drone aerial of the current site — the before

Wide shot of the empty or vacant property (industrial buildings, cracked lots) so the viewer feels the wasted potential.

Tools: Drone

3

AI-render overlay on the aerial — the after

AI-generated visuals laid over the drone shot showing the transformed complex: a coffee shop with big windows and outdoor seating, a brunch spot next door, an Italian restaurant for date night. The render is the wow beat — the same trick as the Kelsie AI home transformation, applied to a whole development instead of one house.

Tools: AI image / video generator

4

Name each concept on screen

One short label per idea, appearing on the beat you say it — never a paragraph on screen.

5

Return to the ground and ask the question

Gesture at the open space and ask viewers what they would want to see there. The question is the engagement engine — it turns a pitch into a conversation in the comments.

6

Close with the branding logo

No hard sell, no phone number on screen — the authority and the question do the work.

Quick wins

Style 6 — Romina's 5 Easy Edits

Romina Aminian · Brand & content strategist

What it looks like: Five small, intentional choices that make a video clearer and easier to follow. Try one or two per video rather than all five at full intensity — the goal is to support what you're saying, not distract from it.

Best for: Making any talking-head reel more watchable, fast — five small changes, no reshoot.

1

Font pairing & placement

Use two complementary fonts with distinct roles: a bold display font for important words, a clean readable font for smaller explanations. Treat text like a graphic — place it where it works with the shot (beside your face, near the object, in negative space). Keep essential text away from Instagram's interface areas.

2

Blank colour screen

Instead of cutting straight from one talking-head shot to another, insert a full-screen solid colour with a short line of text. A brief visual reset that helps a key sentence land.

3

Music that matches the energy

Pick music that fits the video's energy — the track should carry the same feeling as the message, not fight it.

4

Sound effects, sparingly

Add SFX on transitions, text reveals and key movements — but don't overdo it. A few well-placed sounds beat a noisy edit.

5

Simple zooms & animations

Simple zooms, text animations and subtle transitions. Small movement keeps the eye engaged without a full rebuild of the edit.

Style 7 — The Overstimulated Mind Process

Overstimulated Mind edit process · 6 steps, 7 days — the build process behind the Sumit style

What it looks like: The real production process behind a polished talking-head edit: hook and setup, masking and roto, B-roll collages, kinetic type, sound and colour polish, review and export. Seven days, start to finish. This is what "mastering a style" actually means.

Best for: Anyone mastering a style end to end — the full 7-day production plan behind a Sumit-style edit.

1
Day 0–1

Hook & setup

Analyse the raw talking-head footage to craft the opening hook. Set up the timeline and frame rate, sync the original speaking audio. Identify the emphasis words in the script. Establish the visual theme: one glow colour, scattered typography, stark grayscale background shifts.

Tools: DaVinci Resolve, Notion, Google Docs

2
Day 1–2

Masking & roto

Isolate the speaker from the background with Roto Brush. Apply the signature animated neon outline around the exact silhouette. Keyframe the background from the natural room to stark white or grayscale. Clean mask edges around hair and hands during fast movements.

Tools: Remotion, GIMP

3
Day 2–3

B-roll & PiP collages

Gather and trim secondary footage (driving, lounging, walking outdoors) for multi-cam sequences. Design picture-in-picture collages: several clips on screen at once, with drop shadows. Extract and prep 3D assets for metaphor cues (e.g. a dartboard icon for discipline).

Tools: DaVinci Resolve, Remotion

4
Day 3–5

Kinetic typography

Animate bold text popping up word by word, synced to the speaker's pacing. Scattered-text effect for key phrases, words placed randomly across the screen. Varied fonts: serif for accents, clean sans-serif for subtitles. Alternate theme colour and white. Scale bounces and rotational keyframes for energy.

Tools: Remotion

5
Day 5–6

SFX & colour polish

Whooshes for collage slide-ins, distinct pops and clicks for every kinetic word reveal. Balance voiceover EQ with the sound design — SFX never overpower speech. Warm colour grade, natural skin tones kept.

Tools: Audacity, Remotion, DaVinci Resolve

6
Day 6–7

Review & export

Final playback review: rapid text animations and PiP collages perfectly timed. Apply revisions. Export H.264 MP4 optimised for social. Organise project files and back up to the cloud.

Tools: ffmpeg, Frame.io, Google Drive

Style 18 — The Ariya Store Tour — Satellite to Showroom

Marché Ariya · Supermarché — BBQ au Charbon, Greenfield Park / La Prairie, QC

What it looks like: A 30–40 second vertical store tour with no presenter and no voiceover. It opens on a Google Earth-style satellite view that zooms all the way down to the storefront, then glides through about ten departments in slow 2–4 second shots — meat, produce, bulk bins, spices, bakery, tagines — each still moving on a gimbal-smooth push-in or aisle track. One address overlay, one logo watermark, one continuous music bed.

Best for: Showing off a shop, supermarket, showroom or any physical place; a calm, polished tour that lets the space sell itself.

  • No presenter, no voiceover — the space is the star.
  • Every shot keeps moving — slow push-in, gentle pan or forward aisle track, gimbal-smooth, no handheld shake.
  • One text overlay only: the address. Everything else readable is physical in-store signage.
1

Shoot vertical 9:16

Vertical portrait, daylight inside and out; warm interior light, saturated colour (turquoise sky, red signage, colourful produce).

2

Open with the satellite zoom

Google Earth-style high aerial of the street grid, descending continuously for about 8 seconds through a thin cloud layer to the store. Fade the address in bold white sans-serif inside a thin white frame at 2–4 seconds; remove it before landing.

Tools: Google Earth / satellite view

3

Land on the storefront

Low oblique drone shot of the storefront, then a straight-on front view of the entrance for 2–3 seconds.

Tools: Drone

4

Glide through the departments

Hard-cut through 10–12 departments in 2–4 second shots: dining area, meat/deli, produce, bulk bins, spices, counter, oil/sauce, bakery, tagines, wide aisle tracks. Every shot keeps moving — slow push-in, gentle pan or forward track.

Tools: Gimbal / drone

5

Keep the watermark on

Small circular logo watermark at bottom-centre through the interior, semi-transparent. Absent from the opening zoom.

6

Add the two flourish moves

One fast radial zoom-blur swoop (over the bulk bins) and one brief outlined highlight on a hero product — the tagine fills the frame with a thin bright-blue outline/glow for under a second, then cuts to it on the shelf.

Tools: Any editor

7

Close on the logo

Full-frame close-up of the countertop logo sign, then a dimmed exterior shot fading toward black. No call-to-action, no hours, phone or website on screen.

8

Lay the music bed

One continuous instrumental track underneath the whole tour. No voiceover, no dialogue, no subtitles. Track unidentified — pick any unobtrusive instrumental bed.
